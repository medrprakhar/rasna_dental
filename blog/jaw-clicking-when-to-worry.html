Jaw clicking: when should you worry?

By the doctors at Rasna Dental Care · 30 October 2026

Many people notice a click in the jaw while eating or yawning and wonder if something is wrong. A click on its own, without pain, is common and often harmless. It's worth getting checked when it comes with pain, stiffness or a jaw that gets stuck.

Why the jaw clicks

Your jaw joint, the temporomandibular joint or TMJ, sits just in front of each ear. A small disc of cartilage cushions it. If that disc slips slightly forward, the jaw has to ride over it as you open your mouth, and you hear a click. Tight chewing muscles, clenching and grinding can all add to it.

When a click is usually nothing to worry about

  • It doesn't hurt
  • Your mouth still opens fully
  • It hasn't changed over months

When to get it checked

  • The click comes with pain in front of the ear or in the face
  • Your mouth doesn't open as wide as it used to
  • The jaw catches or locks, even for a moment
  • You get headaches or ear pain along with the clicking
  • The click started after an injury to the face

If your jaw has locked and won't open, see a dentist soon. The earlier it's treated, the easier it usually is.

What you can do at home

  • Eat softer food for a while and cut it into small pieces
  • Skip chewing gum and very wide yawns
  • Notice if you clench your teeth during the day, and let your jaw rest with the teeth slightly apart
  • A warm compress on the side of the face can ease sore muscles

If this doesn't help within two weeks, come in for a check-up. Most jaw problems get better with simple treatment such as a night splint. Only a few need a procedure.
