Tooth extraction and wisdom tooth removal in Chittorgarh

चित्तौड़गढ़ में दाँत निकालना और अक़ल दाढ़ का इलाज

We always try to save a tooth first. But sometimes a tooth is too broken, too loose or causing too much trouble, and taking it out is the right choice. Wisdom teeth that come in crooked or get stuck under the gum are the most common reason.

Every extraction starts with an examination and, where needed, an X-ray, so we know the shape of the roots before we begin.

When a tooth may need to come out

  • The tooth is broken or decayed so badly that it can't be restored
  • Gum disease has made it very loose
  • A wisdom tooth is stuck, keeps getting infected, or is damaging the tooth next to it
  • Teeth need to be removed to make space for braces
  • A milk tooth hasn't fallen out and is blocking the permanent tooth

Wisdom teeth

Wisdom teeth usually come in between 17 and 25. Many come in straight and cause no trouble, and those can stay. Others come in at an angle or stay partly under the gum. Food gets trapped around them, the gum swells, and the pain can spread to the ear or make it hard to open the mouth.

An OPG X-ray shows us the angle of the tooth and how close its roots are to the nerve. If it's stuck in the bone, it's removed with a minor surgical procedure under local anaesthesia, at the clinic.

What happens, step by step

  1. 1 Examination and X-rayWe check the tooth, the gums and your medical history, and take an X-ray if needed.
  2. 2 NumbingThe area is numbed with local anaesthesia. You'll feel pressure, but not sharp pain.
  3. 3 RemovalMost teeth come out in a few minutes. A stuck wisdom tooth may take longer and may need a few stitches.
  4. 4 AftercareYou bite on gauze to stop the bleeding, and we give you written instructions and any medicines you need.

After the extraction

  • Bite firmly on the gauze for 30 to 45 minutes.
  • Don't spit, rinse hard or use a straw for the first day. This protects the blood clot.
  • Eat soft, cool food for a day or two, and chew on the other side.
  • Avoid smoking and tobacco for at least a few days. They slow healing and can cause a painful dry socket.
  • Some swelling for two or three days is normal. Call us if bleeding doesn't stop or pain gets worse after the third day.

Tell us before the extraction if you take blood thinners, have diabetes, heart problems or high blood pressure, or are pregnant. We may need to plan around these.

Common questions

Does tooth extraction hurt?

The area is numbed, so you feel pressure rather than pain during the removal. Some soreness afterwards is normal and settles with the medicines we prescribe.

Should every wisdom tooth be removed?

No. A wisdom tooth that has come in straight, can be cleaned and isn't causing problems can stay. We remove the ones that are stuck, keep getting infected, or damage the tooth next to them.

How long does healing take?

The gum usually closes over in one to two weeks. The bone underneath takes a few months to fill in fully, but you won't notice that.

What should I do about the gap?

If it's a back tooth or a front tooth, a gap can cause the nearby teeth to shift. We'll talk about an implant, bridge or denture once the area heals. Wisdom teeth don't need replacing.
